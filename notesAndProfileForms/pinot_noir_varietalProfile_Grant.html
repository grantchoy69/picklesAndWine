Pinot Noir — Varietal Profile

Overview

Pinot Noir is a thin-skinned red grape most closely associated with Burgundy, France. It thrives in cool climates and is famous for expressing small differences in vineyard and vintage. The classic style combines vivid red fruit, lively acidity, gentle tannins, and savory earthiness in a wine that favors nuance over sheer weight.

Core Characteristics

  • Primary Flavors: Red cherry, strawberry, raspberry, cranberry, rose, mushroom, forest floor, clove, vanilla
  • Body: Light to medium
  • Tannin: Low to medium
  • Acidity: Medium-high to high
  • Sweetness: Dry

Aroma & Flavor

Aroma Profile: Fresh red cherry, strawberry, and raspberry, often joined by rose petals, dried herbs, mushroom, damp earth, or forest floor. Oak-aged examples may add clove, vanilla, cedar, or gentle smoke.

Flavor Profile: Bright red fruit carried by refreshing acidity and fine, soft tannins. Cooler-climate wines tend toward tart cherry, cranberry, and savory earth; warmer expressions can show riper strawberry, black cherry, and sweet spice.

Origin & Style

Common Regions: Burgundy (France); Willamette Valley (Oregon); Sonoma Coast, Russian River Valley, and Santa Lucia Highlands (California); Central Otago and Marlborough (New Zealand); Tasmania and Yarra Valley (Australia)

Winemaking Notes: Pinot Noir’s thin skins and delicate aromatics favor gentle extraction. Producers may use whole clusters for spice and structure, neutral or French oak for texture, and cool fermentation to preserve fragrance. It is especially sensitive to climate, site, and handling.

Pairing & Service

Best With: Roast chicken, duck, salmon, pork, mushroom dishes, grilled vegetables, charcuterie, and soft or nutty cheeses

Serve Temperature: 55–60°F

Decant Time: 15–30 minutes for a young bottle; delicate older wines are usually better poured without extended decanting

Aging Potential: 3–10 years for many examples; top wines can develop for 15–25+ years

Vibes / Personality

Observant, graceful, and quietly expressive—the person at the table who never raises their voice, but keeps revealing something more interesting each time you return to the conversation.

Personal Notes

Use Pinot Noir to practice separating fruit ripeness from body. Pay particular attention to the balance among acidity, delicate tannin, red fruit, earth, and oak as the wine opens in the glass.
