Pinot Grigio — Varietal Profile

Overview

Pinot Grigio is a light-bodied white grape originating from Italy's Alto Adige region, prized for its crisp acidity, subtle fruit, and refreshing style. It produces easy-drinking wines with green apple and citrus notes, ideal for warm-weather sipping and versatile pairings.

Core Characteristics

  • Primary Flavors: Green apple, pear, lemon, lime, white peach, almond, minerality
  • Body: Light to medium
  • Tannin: Low (minimal in whites)
  • Acidity: High
  • Sweetness: Dry

Aroma & Flavor

Aroma Profile: Bright citrus and stone fruit, with floral hints of acacia and subtle green apple; cooler-climate versions show more minerality.

Flavor Profile: Crisp and clean, with zesty lemon-lime and pear leading to a mineral finish; some styles add almond or tropical nuances from warmer regions.

Origin & Style

Common Regions: Alto Adige, Friuli-Venezia Giulia (Italy); California (Central Coast); Oregon; Alsace (France, as Pinot Gris, often richer)

Winemaking Notes: Harvested early for acidity; stainless steel fermentation preserves freshness, though some oaked versions add creaminess; Italian styles lean lean and mineral, New World more fruit-forward.

Pairing & Service

Best With: Seafood (grilled fish, shrimp), salads, light cheeses (goat, feta), vegetable dishes, sushi

Serve Temperature: 45–50°F (chilled but not ice-cold)

Decant Time: None needed; serve immediately

Aging Potential: 1–3 years; best young and vibrant

Vibes / Personality

Effortlessly refreshing and approachable—light-hearted, uncomplicated, the reliable companion for sunny afternoons.

Personal Notes

A great entry to white wines; practice spotting acidity's role in cutting through richer foods for balance.
